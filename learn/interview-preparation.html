A Five-Day Job Interview Preparation Plan

Confidence comes from knowing your examples and practising how to explain them. This five-day plan works for many entry-level and experienced roles.

Day 1 — Understand the role

Read the job description carefully. Highlight the responsibilities, tools, work setting and essential requirements. Review the employer's official website for its products, customers and role expectations.

  • Write three reasons the role interests you.
  • Match each key requirement to a real example.
  • Note areas where you would need training.

Day 2 — Prepare your story bank

Choose four experiences: solving a problem, working with others, handling a dissatisfied person and learning something difficult. Use the STAR format.

STAR framework

Situation: What was happening?
Task: What were you responsible for?
Action: What did you personally do?
Result: What changed, and what did you learn?

Day 3 — Practise common questions

Record short spoken answers. Aim for clear, specific explanations rather than scripted perfection.

  1. Tell me about yourself.
  2. Why do you want this role?
  3. Describe a difficult situation you resolved.
  4. How do you prioritise competing tasks?
  5. What is one skill you are developing?
  6. Tell me about a time you received feedback.

Day 4 — Simulate the interview

Ask a friend or AI assistant to interview you one question at a time. After each answer, check whether your example is genuine, understandable and relevant.

Practice prompt

“Interview me for an operations coordinator role. Ask eight questions one by one. Challenge vague answers. Give concise feedback on clarity, evidence and relevance. Never write fictional achievements for me.”

Day 5 — Final readiness check

  • Confirm time, time zone, location or video link.
  • Prepare a copy of the job description and CV.
  • Test your microphone and internet if remote.
  • Prepare two informed questions for the interviewer.
  • Know how to explain your experience honestly.

After the interview, note what went well and what you would improve. Follow up professionally when appropriate.
